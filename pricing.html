Why Mediation Costs Less Than Litigation

One of the first questions people ask is, "How much does mediation cost?" We believe in complete transparency — you deserve to understand the investment before you commit. The short answer: mediation typically costs 70–90% less than going to court.

Why Mediation Costs Less Than Litigation

Traditional litigation is expensive by design. Two attorneys, court filings, discovery, depositions, hearings, and trial preparation add up quickly — often over many months or even years. Mediation streamlines the process by putting both parties in the same (virtual) room with one neutral professional, working together toward resolution.

How to Save on Costs

Every case is unique, and several factors influence the total cost — many of them within your control:

Complexity of issues

A straightforward divorce with few assets will cost less than one involving multiple properties, business interests, retirement accounts, and custody arrangements.

Number of sessions needed

Simple cases may resolve in two to three sessions. More complex matters may require four to six sessions. Each session typically lasts two to three hours.

Level of preparation

Clients who arrive with organized financial documents and a clear sense of their priorities tend to move through the process more efficiently, which directly reduces cost.

Degree of conflict

While mediation is designed to manage conflict, cases where parties are very far apart on key issues may require additional sessions or caucus time.

Cooperation between parties

When both parties are committed to the process and willing to negotiate in good faith, mediation moves faster and costs less.

Payment Options

We understand that the financial pressure of a dispute makes affordability important. Compass Mediation Group offers flexible payment arrangements:

  • Shared cost — Most commonly, both parties split the mediation fees equally.
  • One-party payment — One party may agree to cover the full cost.
  • Custom arrangements — Fees can be divided based on income or any other arrangement the parties agree upon.

Payment details are discussed during your initial consultation, and there are never surprise charges.

The True Cost of Not Mediating

When people compare mediation and litigation costs, they often focus on attorney fees alone. But the full cost of going to court extends much further:

  • Time away from work for court appearances, attorney meetings, and depositions
  • Emotional and mental health costs from prolonged adversarial proceedings
  • Impact on children who are caught in the middle of contentious court battles
  • Damaged relationships that could have been preserved through cooperative resolution
  • Public court records that expose private family and financial details
  • Loss of control — a judge decides your future, not you

Mediation addresses all of these hidden costs by keeping the process private, efficient, and collaborative.
